# INOK user-experience review, 2026-11-20

Scope: the whole route inventory (112 page routes), reviewed as a small business owner on a phone. Method: a repeatable audit (`platform/tests/audit/ux-audit.spec.ts`, run with `playwright.audit.config.ts` against a local clean stack) plus a manual look at screenshots of the main journeys. Local data only (demo seed plus one synthetic agreement with booking terms and a reported issue). Nothing was created in production.

## What the audit measured
333 visits = 110 routes as six kinds of user (visitor, brand-new user, seller, buyer, supplier partner, INOK staff), in English, Malay and Tamil, at 360, 390, 768, 1280 and 1440 px. Per visit: HTTP status, final URL, sideways scroll, visible h1 count, page title, images without alt, links or buttons without a name, untranslated message keys on screen, tap targets under 36 px on phones, console errors, serious or critical axe findings.
Result: no sideways scroll at any width, no untranslated keys, no serious or critical axe findings, no page without a title or h1, no console errors. The only flags were three 404s that are correct (a buyer opening another buyer's request) and one false positive. So the structural quality is good; the real problems found were visual and task-level (below).

## Problems found and fixed in this PR
| # | Page | Problem (phone) | Impact | Root cause | Fix |

| 1 | Wholesale request (`/sourcing/new`), supplier request templates, quotation editor fields | "Product", "Unit" and "City" boxes rendered as tiny unstyled boxes at the left while neighbours were full-width and rounded | High: this is the entry form for bulk buying; looks broken and the boxes are hard to tap | The base CSS styled `input[type=text]` etc. but not inputs with no `type` attribute (valid HTML, means text) | `input:not([type])` added to the base input rule: every typeless input in the app is now styled |
| 2 | Conversation (`/enquiries/[id]`) | Title squeezed into a narrow column next to the "View agreement" button; meta text broke into four short lines | Medium: the main transaction screen looked cramped | Header is one flex row of avatar, title and button | On phones the button takes its own full-width row |
| 3 | Business profile editor (`/orgs/[id]/business/listing`) | "Where do you offer it?" was one column of 17 checkboxes per service (about 700 px tall each) | Medium: very long form for a low-literacy user | Single-column list | Two-column grid: page is about 11% shorter and states are easier to scan |

## Not changed on purpose (recommendations, need a decision or are larger)
Priority order, genuinely useful first:
1. **Enable daily backups or point-in-time recovery on the production database.** Today only manual dumps exist. (Owner decision, plan cost.)
2. **Decide the erasure policy for agreements (option C, subject to legal and privacy review)** before real use: an organisation with an agreement cannot be erased yet. See `docs/erasure-of-agreements.md`.
3. **Operational readiness before a real-business pilot:** transactional email (confirmation, recovery, notifications), the verification standard and staff procedure, the malware scanner for uploads, a written fraud/complaint procedure.
4. **Business 360 is still long on phones** even with next actions first: consider showing only readiness, next actions and numbers, with the rest behind "More".
5. **Pre-fill a revised quotation** from the previous version (the editor starts empty after a revision request, so the seller retypes everything).
6. **Seller-side response to a reported issue** (reply or acknowledge) is not built; staff can read the issue but there is no resolution workflow, by design until the payment partner is chosen.
7. **Directory "Reviewed only" switch** is 28 px tall (small for a thumb); other tap targets are acceptable (checkboxes sit inside larger labels).
8. **Tab bars** (My deals) scroll sideways on phones and cut a label ("My reque..."): shorter tab names or a two-row layout would help.
9. **Escrow remains separate:** quotations and agreements are structured so a payment provider can be added later (agreement status never says paid or protected). Not built, as instructed.

## Not verified here
Signed-in screens on the real production site (no production accounts were created). Production verification is limited to public pages and route behaviour; signed-in journeys are covered by the local and CI end-to-end tests.
